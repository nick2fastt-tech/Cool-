Update Log

V1.9 beta
  • New map: The Mines. 30 waves underground — rock ceiling, pit props, rails with carts sat on them, strung lamps for light, and the track running straight, right, left, then hard right into a hole in the floor. Unlocked by clearing The Volcano
  • 13 enemies found only down there. Waves 1–10 are the crew that went down: Miners, Drifters, Lamp Carriers, Cave Crawlers, Shaft Diggers, Stone Zombies, and The Foreman at wave 10. 70 to 1,500 HP
  • Waves 11–30 are what the deep workings sent back: Pickaxe Zombie, Crack Source, Slow Pickaxe, Brute Stone, and the Fire Source and Zombie Tank as mini bosses. 400 to 3,500 HP. The Gold Miner is waiting at wave 30 with 10,000
  • Stone zombies carry shields that stop bullets dead. Scout, Sniper, Blitzer, Robber, Survivor and Toy Soldier do nothing to them — bring blades, a baton or fire
  • The Mines pays $150 on wave 1 up to $7,000 on wave 30. You will need it. Clear it once and you keep a 5,000 coin prize
  • The Volcano no longer lags. It was carrying 66 lights — one per lava crack, pool and vent — and in a forward renderer every light is computed on every pixel of everything. Down to 15, with one shared light that jumps to whichever vent is erupting. Its frame render went from 166ms to 21ms
  • Fixed: The Forest had a second Grave Digger on wave 15. It has exactly one boss now, at wave 20
  • The Sniper is locked and costs $750 to deploy — bought once with coins, like the Commander
  • The Mine tower pays every wave too, on top of its five-second trickle: $150 a wave at Lv1 up to $2,000 at Lv5
  • The Blocky Event runs for two weeks from the first time you open this build, then it closes. The countdown is on the Event button
V1.85
  • The black flicker is gone. It was mine: the frame-rate governor defended itself by lowering the render resolution, which reallocates the drawing buffer — and a reallocation shows as a black frame. It only ever happened in a crowd because that is exactly when the governor acted. It now buys the time by pulling detail distances in instead, and the buffer is only touched when you change quality
  • Crowds cost far less. Health bars and numbers were two extra see-through draws per zombie — about 180 of them with ninety on the field. They now only draw for things close enough to read, bosses, or anything actually hurt: 1,862 draw calls down to 1,422, and HP repaints from 123 a second to none
  • Effects are capped, so twenty towers firing into a wave cannot flood the frame with sparks
  • New: The Sniper. $500, stays crouched behind a scoped bolt-action with quad-tube night vision. 7 damage at Lv1 up to 50 at Lv4, one shot every 3s down to 1.5s, and a real 2-second bolt cycle after every shot where it cannot fire
  • The Burned Corpse has no weapon. It was falling through to the default pose, which braces the arms as if holding a pistol. It has its own stance now — hunched and slack, rearing back to draw breath, thrown forward with the flame
  • The Volcano erupts. Vents fire columns of flame on their own clocks and throw lava bombs that arc out and fall back. Lava runs in glowing cracks across the ground, and embers pour up off the hot rock
  • The Event, rebuilt. One long open run — six waypoints instead of fourteen doubling back — so there is somewhere to build. The UI is bricks: studded red, green and yellow panels
  • Brick things move like bricks. Knees locked, rocking off each straight leg, elbows fixed. The Bloater gets a heavier version, leaning back over its belly and planting each foot
  • The Bloater clamps your towers shut. Two brick jaws close over the gun and it cannot fire for 4.5 seconds while it shakes them off. Two or three towers at a time, every nine seconds, within 13 units
V1.80
  • Real textures. The ground, patches and path used to be single flat colours — that is why it looked like plastic. They now have painted grain: grass with individual blades, dirt with grit, pebbles and dragged ruts, rock with pitting and hairline cracks
  • Normal-mapped relief. Every texture carries a matching bump map derived from its own detail, so the pebbles in the path genuinely catch the light on the side the sun is on instead of being a flat picture
  • Proper colour pipeline. The renderer now works in linear light and encodes to sRGB with a filmic curve on the way out — highlights roll off instead of clipping and shadows keep their detail. Every colour in the game was converted so the art looks as it was drawn, just lit properly
  • No pop-in stutter. Shaders are compiled up front when a map loads, so the first time something new appears it does not freeze the game while the browser builds a program
  • Surfaces are lit properly but deliberately not full PBR — measured, the physically-based shader cost 50ms a frame more for no visible gain on rough ground, so they use the cheaper path. Only the few dozen big surfaces are textured at all; characters stay on the cheap material
  • Texture tiling is cached rather than cloned per object: that alone went from 177 GPU textures down to 52
  • The relief is High-only and Low drops textures' bump entirely, so each setting really is cheaper than the one above it. At Medium the game now runs faster than V1.70 did — 46% fewer draw calls — while looking considerably better
  • No image files — every texture is drawn at startup, so the game is still one file that works offline
V1.75
  • The Blocky Event is live. 20 waves of bricks and loose pieces — 11 types, from Stud Runners to Stack Towers — and a Blocky Bloater every fifth wave: 500 HP on wave 5, 1,000 on 10, 1,500 on 15, 2,000 on 20
  • New: Toy Soldier, the event reward. $450. Starts almost useless at 1 damage every 0.5s and ends up the fastest gun in the game — 15 a shot every 0.09s, 167 dps. Range 7 → 14. Upgrades $200 → $700 → $2,500
  • Runs a lot faster. Identical shapes now share one geometry instead of allocating their own: a busy scene went from 4,931 geometries to 554. Tiny parts stop drawing at distance, and only meshes big enough to see cast shadows. Draw calls down 37%, triangles down 44%
  • It holds its frame rate. The renderer quietly trades a little resolution when a scene gets heavy and takes it back when it clears, so High stays High and stops stuttering
  • Tower animations rebuilt. Weight rolls from foot to foot with the loaded hip lifting and the light heel coming up, the head leads the turn and tips down onto anything close, and recoil now travels through the hips and knees instead of only moving the arms
  • Playlist picker in the lobby. ♫ Music lists every song with its length, you pick up to 30, and it shows the total running time. Your choice is saved
