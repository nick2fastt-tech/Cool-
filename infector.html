INFECTOR — an online 3D multiplayer infection-survival game.

Everything you see runs from a single HTML file: no engine, no images, no downloads. Geometry, characters, animation, particles, lighting and every sound are generated at runtime.

Engine
Custom WebGL renderer, batched draw calls, packed-depth shadow maps, dynamic resolution
Characters
Procedural stickman rig with 20-bone hierarchy and weighted animation blending
Audio
Fully synthesized via Web Audio: wind, birds, growls, footsteps, adaptive score
Netcode
Authoritative round simulation with snapshot interpolation & client prediction
Maps
Playground, Neighborhood & the three-storey Hotel — procedurally assembled

V1.21 — THE ELEVATOR

Animation overhaul. Every walk, run, sprint and shamble is now solved against the real leg geometry instead of hand-tuned by eye. The pelvis drops and rises exactly as far as the two-link leg chain demands, so the planted foot sits on the floor through the whole stride — and the cycle rate is derived from the swing amplitude, so the feet cover the ground the body actually travels. Stride length is up 33% on walking and 55% on sprinting, and a floor clamp in the pose pipeline means no pose can push a sole through the ground again.

A real elevator. The lift no longer wanders on a timer. There is a call button at every landing and a three-floor panel inside the car; press E (or the on-screen button) to use them. Buttons light while a call is pending, the floor readout tracks the car, the doors slide, and the ride is timed to take 11–15 seconds door to door — with an ease in and out so it starts and stops like a real car. The HUD counts down your arrival while you ride.

The hotel, furnished. Ceiling light panels on every floor and in every room, wall sconces and numbered doors down the corridors, a carpet runner and a housekeeping trolley. Reception gained a key-pigeonhole wall, a clock, a lamp and a bell; the lounge gained a rug, a coffee table, planters and a luggage cart, with vending machines by the lift. Guest rooms gained a dresser and television, a desk and chair, a wardrobe, curtains, a rug and a picture over the beds.

V1.20 — NIGHTMARE HOTEL

Four difficulties. Every room now runs at a set difficulty and the servers list lets you filter by it. Easy is a 90-second round with gentler infected. Normal is the standard 2½-minute outbreak. Hard runs 3½ minutes against faster, hungrier zombies. Insane is five minutes, 15–20 players and three carriers turned loose at once.

The Hotel. A three-storey building you play the inside of: a lobby with a front desk, dining room, laundry and storage; two guest floors of rooms off a central corridor; and a roof. Stairwells at both ends, and a working elevator that closes its doors, travels between floors and carries whoever is standing in the car.

Hide under the beds. Get close to a bed and press F (or the HIDE button) to drag yourself underneath. The camera drops to mattress height, you can still shuffle around at a third of your speed, and most zombies will walk straight past and search another room — but a Crawler is already down there with you and will come straight in.

Doors. Every guest room door is dealt open or shut at the start of the round, so the routes through a floor change every time. A shut door is solid until someone shoves through it, and it makes a noise when they do.

V1.12 — THE CRAWLER

A third zombie class that never gets up. The Crawler drags itself along the ground at 3.6 m/s — half the speed of anything else out there — so it has to ambush. Instead of going for your throat it snaps at your ankles, and if it connects your legs go out from under you and you land on your back.

Then it climbs on top and feeds. Blood pools and spreads under the body, wounds open on the shoulder and stomach, and it tears at you in a rhythm for about seven seconds before moving on. Other zombies drift over and join in.

You are on the floor for thirty seconds. You fight it at first, then weaken, then go still while the infection takes hold — and at the end you roll over, push yourself up and roar as one of them.

V1.11 — REALISM & CHARACTER OVERHAUL

One infection system — bites and scratches now run the same clock, exactly one minute, in three stages. 0–20s: you react, breathe hard and hold the wound, but you can still run. 20–40s: you slow down, stumble, drip green, and the zombies lose interest in you. 40–60s: your legs go, you lower yourself to the ground and cannot stand, your breathing goes shallow and your eyes close. Then you go still, twitch, open them changed, push yourself up and roar onto the infected team.

Faces — real eyelids that sweep over the eyeball instead of scaling it, a brow ridge, nose bridge and tip, cheekbones and a chin, plus separate upper and lower lips that shape with the expression.

Bodies — deltoid caps instead of ball shoulders, hands with four fingers and a thumb that curl into a fist when you run or strike, and fabric folds across the chest and waist that shift with the body.

Movement — the idle shifts weight onto one leg and back with the hips tipping and the loaded knee locking, and the walk rolls heel-strike to toe-off instead of holding a flat foot.

Feeding — with nobody left to chase, zombies drift toward a downed survivor and gather to feed.

V1.10 — THE INFECTION EVOLUTION

Two classes — every zombie is now a Walker (slow, relentless, never loses your trail, heavy breathing and a limping gait) or a Sprinter (explosive bursts, twitchy scanning idle, long pumping strides — and it throws itself shoulder-first into fences and cars when it misjudges a corner, losing a second to a stumbling recovery).

Line-up cutscene — every round opens with the players shoulder to shoulder while the camera tracks the line, settles on one of them with a sting, and the match starts.

Patient Zero — nobody carries a syringe any more. The first infected has one lodged in their upper back, glowing, for the whole round. Everyone else turns without one.

Bites and scratches — a clean bite from behind takes you straight down. Caught face-on and you twist away with a scratch instead: you keep playing, but you slow down, stumble, bring up green, pale out and your eyes drain over about a minute — then you fall, push yourself up and roar as a zombie. Other zombies leave you alone while you turn.

Bigger maps — the playground gained a climbing tower with a net and tunnel, a second swing set, a soccer pitch, picnic tables and far more trees. The neighbourhood gained side streets, four more intersections, garages and driveways, a convenience store and a bus stop.

V1.02 — MOVEMENT & MOBILITY

Joystick — rebuilt for true 360° control with a rescaled dead zone, snappier acceleration, extra bite when you reverse, rate-limited turning so the character always faces where it is going, and sprint on a full forward push. Touches that begin on a HUD button no longer steer you.

Jumping — a standing jump loads with a knee bend and an arm swing, legs tuck in the air, longer jumps lean into the arc, and landings absorb through the knees before blending straight back into a walk or run. Dust kicks up on every landing.

Auto climb — walk into a fence, porch, bonnet or car roof and your stickman vaults or mantles it automatically. Hands reach the lip first, a knee drives up onto the edge, the trailing leg follows and the body stands up, blending back into movement. Destinations are checked for footing and headroom, so nobody climbs into a wall or through a roof.

Infected variants — the first infected of a round may be a Runner (faster, quick strikes, long sprint) or a Brute (bigger, slower, with a swing that knocks everyone nearby off their feet, heavy footsteps and a much deeper roar). Everyone infected afterwards becomes a Regular.

Progression — coins are gone. Cosmetics unlock by levelling up and by clearing milestones, and everything lives on a profile you sign in to.

V1.01 — HUMAN DETAIL & ZOMBIE TRANSFORMATION

Humans — reworked male and female builds with rounder hands and feet and better limb proportions. Blinking, and a full expression set (smile, scared, shocked, angry, nervous) that eases between moods. Heads turn to track what matters and lead the direction you run. Deeper idle breathing with shoulder movement, natural running arm swing, a bounce in the walk, smoother jump take-off and landing, cloth lag on hoodies, coats and skirts, and foot placement that plants on kerbs, benches and car roofs.

Transformation — a syringe to the upper back freezes the victim for about a second, they clutch the wound, the change spreads across the body while they shake, they bend forward and rise as a zombie with a roar. Clothing, hair, hats and accessories are kept exactly as equipped.

Zombies — glowing eyes, sharper teeth, a heavier hunch, idle twitching, dedicated turning animation and a more aggressive run.

Fixes — no more floating above props, clothing clipping, spawning inside another player, bots wedged on scenery, jump animation failing to reset, foot sliding while running, survivor faces surviving infection, camera clipping through walls, or getting stuck after turning. Sharper syringe hit detection, throttled effects during large infection chains, and a cheaper allocation-free animation blender.

Built with Claude Code. Have fun, and watch your back.
