INFECTOR — an online 3D multiplayer infection-survival game.

Everything you see runs from a single HTML file: no engine, no images, no downloads. Geometry, characters, animation, particles, lighting and every sound are generated at runtime.

Engine
Custom WebGL renderer, batched draw calls, packed-depth shadow maps, dynamic resolution
Characters
Procedural stickman rig with 20-bone hierarchy and weighted animation blending
Audio
Fully synthesized via Web Audio: wind, birds, growls, footsteps, adaptive score
Netcode
Authoritative round simulation with snapshot interpolation & client prediction
Maps
Playground & Neighborhood — procedurally assembled, bright sunny lighting

V1.01 — HUMAN DETAIL & ZOMBIE TRANSFORMATION

Humans — reworked male and female builds with rounder hands and feet and better limb proportions. Blinking, and a full expression set (smile, scared, shocked, angry, nervous) that eases between moods. Heads turn to track what matters and lead the direction you run. Deeper idle breathing with shoulder movement, natural running arm swing, a bounce in the walk, smoother jump take-off and landing, cloth lag on hoodies, coats and skirts, and foot placement that plants on kerbs, benches and car roofs.

Transformation — a syringe to the upper back freezes the victim for about a second, they clutch the wound, the change spreads across the body while they shake, they bend forward and rise as a zombie with a roar. Clothing, hair, hats and accessories are kept exactly as equipped.

Zombies — glowing eyes, sharper teeth, a heavier hunch, idle twitching, dedicated turning animation and a more aggressive run.

Fixes — no more floating above props, clothing clipping, spawning inside another player, bots wedged on scenery, jump animation failing to reset, foot sliding while running, survivor faces surviving infection, camera clipping through walls, or getting stuck after turning. Sharper syringe hit detection, throttled effects during large infection chains, and a cheaper allocation-free animation blender.

Built with Claude Code. Have fun, and watch your back.
